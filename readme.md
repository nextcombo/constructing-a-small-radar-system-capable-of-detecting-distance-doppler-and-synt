# Constructing a small radar system capable of detecting distance, Doppler, and synthetic aperture radar imaging [with related resources]

MIT Open Source Radar Course Sharing: Build a Small Radar System for Distance Measurement, Doppler Analysis, and SAR Imaging

Want to delve deeper into radar technology and even build a small radar system capable of distance detection, Doppler analysis, and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imaging? The MIT open-source course is definitely the perfect fit for your interest in radar technology!
Course highlights: A complete radar journey from theory to practice
This course was launched by MIT in January 2011 (course number: RES-LL-003), focusing on "learning by doing". It takes you from basic radar concepts all the way to building your own radar and conducting field experiments.
•
Knowledge comprehensive: From radar fundamentals, antenna design, modular radio frequency system design to SAR imaging, four lectures thoroughly cover the core technologies of radar (some lectures provide PDF handouts, which can be directly accessed on the course page).
•
Practical attributes maximized: Teams of three start from scratch to build a small radar system based on an FMCW architecture operating in the S band, and also use it to conduct fun experiments such as "measuring car speed", "plotting moving target range", and "SAR imaging competition".
•
Resources are completely open source: The radar system design diagrams, schematics, material lists, manufacturing instructions, as well as the MATLAB codes and example files needed for experiments are freely available, greatly reducing the barrier to entry!
Course structure: Clear learning path with lecture modules (theoretical solidification)

Project Module (Practice Breakthrough) Students will team up to complete the full process of the "From Radar Hardware Construction to Field Experiments" project:
•
Radar Functionality: Based on the coherent FMCW architecture, using coffee cans as both transmitters and receivers, paired with micro-circuit components, four-way operational amplifiers, etc., ultimately achieving three working modes: Doppler and Time, Distance and Time, SAR Imaging.
•
Experiment Support: Each experiment is accompanied by MATLAB code and example files (such as .wav, .tif, .mat, etc.), helping you complete data processing and analysis.

Participation Requirements and Benefits
•
Prerequisites: You must have a laptop equipped with MATLAB installed and have some MATLAB experience; if you are interested in electronics, amateur radio, physics, or electromagnetism, learning will be smoother.
•
Skill Gains: Through hands-on construction of radar and completion of a series of experiments, you will gain intuitive understanding of application electromagnetics, radio frequency technology, analog circuits, signal processing, and other engineering topics, as well as the sense of achievement of "making radar work", achieving self-motivated learning!
